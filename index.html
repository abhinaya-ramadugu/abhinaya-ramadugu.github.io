TORK damper · Siemens Gamesa · 2020

Tunable vibration absorber for wind turbine blades

My role

Mechanical design lead and financial manager, ideation through precision fabrication and test, in a team of three. Named inventor on US 2024/0426273 A1.

The problem

Longer blades carry very little inherent damping, so resonant response builds instead of dying out and drives fatigue. The sponsor set scaled targets on a two meter cantilevered beam: at least 20 percent logarithmic decrement, under 5 percent of beam mass, under 17 cm tall, working in any orientation.

The design

A rack and pinion converts the linear motion of a small mass into rotation of a heavy flywheel, so the device delivers far more effective inertia than a linear damper of the same mass. Tuning comes from three independent parameters rather than one: linear mass, flywheel inertia, and pinion radius. Over 50 components, 21 of them custom.

Designing for cost

Machined Delrin carriage slotted for mass reduction, a brass flywheel carrying up to 18 tungsten rods for tunable inertia, four music wire springs, and a welded steel plate exciter rig built to excite the beam at constant force across the test range. The material choices cut carriage mass by 35 percent while keeping inertia tunable.

Result. 21.00 percent logarithmic decrement in the vertical orientation against a 20 percent target, 18.97 percent inverted, 15.89 percent horizontal. Measured frequency response matched the SIMULINK model closely at ideal tuning. When spring rate verification found the delivered springs 17.7 percent below target, I recovered it by removing eight tungsten inserts to retune the system rather than reordering custom springs, protecting both budget and schedule.

Rotating 3D scanner · Medtronic · 2021

Contactless registration for prone cranial surgery

My role

Mechanical and PCB design engineer. Named inventor on US Patent 12,579,737 B2, published as WO 2023/150038 A1.

The problem

Cranial navigation only works if the patient's real head position is matched to their CT or MRI scan. Registration is normally done by touching points on the face with a probe, but in prone surgery the face is clamped facing down with very little room underneath, which makes that slow, awkward, and easy to disturb once the patient is positioned.

What I designed

A motor driven scanner arm that clamps to the surgical table beside the head clamp and sweeps a LiDAR scanner beneath the face through an arc of up to 120 degrees, from the top of the face down to the chin. I set the arm geometry and joint angle for clearance and standoff, selected the motor and transmission, laid out the drive electronics on a single layer board in Eagle, and wrote the control code.

Result. A working prototype delivered within its $2,000 budget and schedule, a granted patent, and a 25 percent cut in part count through DFM and FMEA during design and build.
